# Recurring Characters (Channel Bible)

Faceless, animated stickman psychology channel. One narrator voice speaks the whole video. Characters never get their own voices; they act, react, and use speech bubbles, stamps and signs. This keeps TTS simple and the cast instantly readable.

## Global style

- Thick rounded lines (about 6px at 1080p), round heads, big expressive eyes and eyebrows.
- Clean off-white background (#F7F5F0), 1 or 2 flat props per scene.
- Character colours below are fixed across every video. Each video also gets one accent colour for highlights and key caption words; it must not clash with a character colour (red #E5484D is the default accent).

## Character notes

### Nim (the viewer)
- Gender-neutral name and design, so any viewer can see themselves in Nim.
- Lives in a small flat: front door, couch, desk with laptop. Most opening scenes start here, so viewers recognise the world in the first second.
- Nim is never stupid or lazy on screen. Nim does what normal people do, and the video explains why.
- Arc in every video: stuck at the start, same situation resolved at the end.

### Auto (the autopilot)
- Represents the brain's fast, automatic processes: habits, predictions, gut reactions, biases.
- Auto is not a villain. It's trying to save energy and keep Nim safe, and it sticks with what worked before.
- Control room set: a panel with levers, a big red NO button, and a filing cabinet with labelled drawers ("EFFORT", "PEOPLE", "MONEY"). New drawers get added as the channel covers new topics, which builds a running visual world across videos.
- Accuracy rule: once per video, the narrator or an on-screen tag makes clear Auto is a cartoon of automatic processes, not a single part of the brain.

### Doc (the research guide)
- Shows up only for studies, so viewers learn that Doc on screen means "this part is backed by research".
- Always holds a Study Card: researcher, year, and what they did (e.g. "Hofmann et al. 2012 / 205 adults / 1 week").
- Doc is fair: when a finding faded or later research questioned it, Doc flips the card to show a "But..." side. This builds trust and matches the channel's accuracy standard.

### Nag (the inner critic)
- Appears in videos about self-talk, guilt, perfectionism, procrastination and comparison.
- Nag never fully disappears. When Nim uses the video's fixes, Nag shrinks to a small puff. This keeps the message honest: the critic gets quieter, it doesn't vanish.

## Recurring devices

| Device | What it looks like | When it appears |
|---|---|---|
| Study Card | Index card held by Doc, accent-colour border | Every research claim |
| "Tonight" sticky note | Yellow sticky note slapped onto the screen | Each practical fix |
| Auto's stamp swap | Auto swaps "NOT TODAY" for "YES" | Final scene of every video |
| Pause beat | Screen freezes, small timer counts 3, 2, 1 | When the narrator asks the viewer a question |

The stamp swap is the channel's signature ending. Regular viewers will start waiting for it.

## How the cast maps to the discipline video

- Nim: comes home, sees the gym bag, sinks into the couch.
- Auto: stamps "NOT TODAY" in the opening, files "FEEL BAD" in the EFFORT drawer, stamps "YES" in the final scene.
- Doc: presents Hofmann, Eisenberger, Schultz, Mueller and Dweck, Milkman, Lally, Ent, and Kahneman. Flips the card to "But..." for the gym audiobook effect fading.
- Nag: rains "compare to last year" and "didn't finish" on Nim in the forced-sessions section, shrinks during the five fixes.
